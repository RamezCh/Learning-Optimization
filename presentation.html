1. Current Status of the Algorithm(s)

Overview of codebase implementations on the regression branch

Production & Research Implementations

The project codebase currently contains three distinct, fully implemented and tested pricing architectures:

  • Master Algorithm (duopoly.py – v5.0):
    Production contest entry. Operates as a closed-loop dynamic pacing controller using an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) price anchor, universal low-sales circuit breaker, duopoly high-price ceiling guard ($52), and empirical monopoly sweet-spot harvester ($52–$56).
  • Multiple Linear Regression Variant (duopoly_mlr.py):
    Online Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) model trained on prior seasons of the active simulation to estimate simulation-specific baseline demand ($\beta_0$) and cross-price sensitivity ($\beta_{\Delta}$), adjusting target anchor prices.
  • Poisson / Logistic Discrete Choice Variant (duopoly_poisson_logit.py):
    Estimates time-dependent Poisson passenger arrival intensity $\lambda(t)$ and logistic discrete choice conversion probability, optimizing ticket price against capacity opportunity cost $\mu(t, c)$.

2. Desired Goals

Mathematical objectives, inventory constraints, and competitive requirements

Primary Optimization Objective

Maximize cumulative total revenue across 100 independent flight departures (selling seasons) under an uncertain, randomized market demand model:

Maximize: Total Revenue = ∑s=1..100 ∑t=1..100 [ ps,t × ds,t ]

Perishable Inventory Constraints

  • Fixed Capacity Constraint: Exactly 80 seats per season. No replenishment possible.
  • Zero Salvage Value: At period $t=100$, any unsold seat expires with exactly $0 salvage value ($V(0) = \$0.00$).
  • Seat Spoilage Target: Average unsold seats at period 100 must be ≤ 1.0 seat per season (≥ 98.7% load factor in stable markets).

Competitive & Behavioral Objectives

  • Monopoly Extraction: When the rival sells out their 80 seats, exploit unconstrained monopoly pricing along empirical consumer reservation price tiers ($72–$92) without threat of undercutting.
  • Adversarial Defense: Defend against extreme competitor strategies:
    • Deep dumpers ($12–$19) without falling into zero-sales holdout traps.
    • Persistent low-price deadlocks ($22–$27) through automated circuit breakers.
    • Copycat / undercutting rivals without initiating a destructive downward spiral.
    • High-price anchor traps ($70–$95) by observing customer reservation ceilings.
  • Academic Alignment: Direct demonstration and evaluation of the Target Depletion Curve, Booking Curve Pacing, and Regression Demand Modeling.

3. Implementation Details

The strategic dilemma, opponent profiling, and decision flow

Core Logic: The Strategic Dilemma

  • Ideal Strategy (Monopoly Induction):
    If the rival prices low, allow them to rapidly burn their 80 seats ($80 \times p_{\text{rival}} \approx \$1,472$). Once they deplete inventory, they exit the market. We become the monopoly seller and harvest remaining demand at $72–$92.
  • Market Reality (The Capacity Trap):
    Total market arrivals average only 75 to 105 passengers across 100 periods, while combined airline capacity is 160 seats. If the rival prices at $35–$55, they will never sell out. Passively waiting leads to stranded seats and zero revenue.
  • Dynamic Response:
    If the rival is not selling out and our booking pace falls behind schedule, the algorithm detects that market demand is insufficient. It immediately shifts from passive holdout to actively contesting volume via dynamic undercutting (-$1.50 to -$8.00).

4. Which Ideas Worked Well and Which Didn't

Real-world failure postmortems and validated engineering solutions from ALGORITHM_CHANGES.md

What Failed (Postmortems & Traps)

  • v1.0 Market Size Fallacy ($0.00 in 4 of 6 Tournaments):
    Assumed arrivals > 160. Held $85 early, raised to $105 in Phase 3. Rivals captured all arrivals. 89.9% of seats spoiled unsold (43,141 seats lost).
  • v2.0 "Death Valley" Trap (FuturisticSalamander at $18.40):
    Undercutting to $28–$35 against an $18 dumper lost both volume and margin. Rival still sold out 80 seats, while we sold few seats at low prices ($980/season).
  • v3.0 Passive Holdout Blowout (35zapi → $0.00 Total Revenue):
    Held $58 against $12 dumper RussetBat. Rival only sold out at period 98; in the remaining 2 periods, customer WTP was below $64. Sold 0 seats across 100 seasons!
  • v4.0 Dumper Deadlock (3d8tDh → 4,342 Spoiled Seats):
    SageSloth priced at median $25.80. Classified as dumper, anchor snapped to $35, price frozen at $27. Rival never sold out, freezing our bot for 100 seasons.
  • Unconstrained Myopic Regression:
    Raising prices based purely on single-period regression caused rivals to undercut, dropping conversion to zero and leaving 10% lower capacity load factor.

What Worked Well (Key Breakthroughs)

  • Universal Low-Sales Circuit Breaker:
    Automatically breaks out of failed holdouts and dumper freezes by contesting volume when sales fall below 42 for 2 seasons. Cured both 35zapi and 3d8tDh.
  • High-Price Ceiling Guard ($52.00):
    When rival prices at $70+, quoting $63–$67 caused demand collapse because customer reservation prices are capped at $50–$85. Cap at $52 cured 3kGhWc 40% seat spoilage.
  • Monopoly Sweet Spot ($52–$56):
    Quoting >$65 in monopoly produced an 80% zero-conversion rate. Pricing high-burn monopoly at $52–$56 yields peak $70.28/period and converts 1.45 seats/period.
  • Continuous Anchor Smoothing ($35–$44):
    Replaced discontinuous anchor cliffs with smooth linear scaling: clip(44 - 0.8*(gap-1.5), 35, 44), preventing undercutting cascades.
  • Unconditional Late Clearance at t ≥ 90:
    Liquidating remaining inventory regardless of gap eliminated perishable seat spoilage.

5. Performance Evaluation: Target Curve, Booking Curve & Regression

Formulations, econometric validation (80/20 split), and 1,500-season benchmark comparison

Target Curve & Booking Pacing

  • Target Depletion Curve: $\text{TargetSold}(t) = 80 \cdot (0.85\tau + 0.15\tau^2)$.
    Convex pacing aligns with accelerating Poisson arrival intensity $\lambda(t)$ near departure, avoiding premature discounting early in the season.
  • Booking Curve Pacing: Real-time Inventory Gap $\text{RemainingCapacity}(t) - \text{TargetRemaining}(t)$ governs dynamic undercuts (-$1.50 to -$8.00) or scarcity premiums (+$1.00 to +$14.00).
